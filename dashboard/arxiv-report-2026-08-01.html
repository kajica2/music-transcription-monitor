The take

MuScriptor is still the most‑impactful paper of the year for AMT‑as‑AMT. But for this week, the headline is the Qwen team using Qwen3‑ASR as the in‑loop lyrics metric for Qwen‑Music — and VocalParse is the open fine‑tune of that same Qwen3‑ASR for SVT. The same model that scores generation quality also serves as the lyrics side of multi‑track AMT. If you're building a music‑aware LLM agent, the new de facto stack is: Qwen3‑ASR / VocalParse for lyrics, MuScriptor for audio→MIDI, MuseAgent‑1's OMR module for score→symbolic. № 01 · Most impactful

MuScriptor — Open Multi‑Instrument AMT Foundation Model

Rouard, Krause, Roebel, Simon‑Gabriel, Défossez · Kyutai × Mirelo · 2026-07-09
Existing methods for AMT are often limited to single‑instrument recordings or fail on complex, real music mixes. We analyze the effectiveness of synthetic data for pre‑training while combining it with fine‑tuning on real music audio and post‑training using reinforcement learning. We further introduce conditioning on instrument presence to customize transcriptions. Finally, we release MuScriptor, an open‑weight multi‑instrument music transcription model that works on real‑world music recordings from across a diverse range of musical genres.

Decoder‑only Transformer reads mel‑spectrogram, autoregressively spits MIDI tokens (MT3‑style). Three‑stage training: synthetic pretrain → real‑audio fine‑tune → RL post‑training, plus instrument‑set conditioning at inference. Beats YourMT3+ by 26 points on multi‑instrument F1 (48.2 vs 21.9). MIT code, CC BY‑NC weights, 941★ on the repo, uvx muscriptor serve ships a web UI. The most‑traction multi‑instrument AMT release of the year.

Code released github.com/muscriptor/muscriptor
№ 02

Qwen‑Music Technical Report

Qwen Team (Jin Xu, Kangdi Wang, Ruibin Yuan, et al.) · Alibaba · v2 2026-07-27 v2 this cycle
We introduce Qwen‑Music, a powerful music generation model capable of producing highly musical and high‑fidelity songs with complete vocal singing. Architecturally, Qwen‑Music integrates three core components: Qwen‑Music‑Tokenizer, Qwen‑Music‑LLM, and Qwen‑Music‑Render. The novelty is a melody‑token‑based chain‑of‑thought (Melody‑CoT) mechanism that plans melodies before full‑song generation. Trained on more than 5 million hours of multilingual music data. State‑of‑the‑art on 13 of 16 objective musicality / audio‑quality metrics.

First closed/open‑weights generation FM to use an AMT model as an in‑loop metric. The lyrics intelligibility of generated songs is scored with Qwen3‑ASR — the same backbone VocalParse is fine‑tuned from. 25 Hz single‑codebook tokenizer, Melody‑CoT trick, 5M hours of training. Tech report, no public code at v2 — watch the Qwen/ Hugging Face org.

Tech report Closed-form release, no public code at v2
№ 03

MulTTiPop — Multitrack Transcription Dataset for Pop Music

Pruyne, Stoler, Chen, Huang, Watanabe, Donahue · CMU + JHU + Adobe · 2026-07-09 New
We present MulTTiPop, a dataset of pop music segments and their associated multitrack MIDI recordings for the evaluation of automatic music transcription models. MulTTiPop contains 572 segments of popular music totaling 3.5 hours of audio, and contains songs from diverse genres and decades from the 1930s to 2000s. We evaluate state‑of‑the‑art AMT models on MulTTiPop and find substantial room for improvement, with the best model achieving 38% Onset F1.

New commercial‑pop AMT eval set. 572 segments, 3.5 hours, tempo‑warped multitrack MIDI aligned to Lakh MIDI + TheoryTab audio. The best SOTA AMT model hits 38% Onset F1 on this set. License CC BY 4.0, eval‑only (don't train on it). The necessary counterweight to the MuScriptor hype.

Dataset + samples gclef-cmu.org/multtipop
№ 04 · Architecture pattern

MuseAgent‑1 — Interactive Grounded Multimodal Understanding of Music Scores + Performance Audio

Zhao, Cao, Huang, Leong, Zhang, Yap, Hu · NTU + BUCT + Microsoft + U. Chicago · 2026-01-17
Despite recent advances in multimodal large language models, their ability to understand and interact with music remains limited. We introduce MuseAgent, a music‑centric multimodal agent that augments language models with structured symbolic representations derived from sheet music images and performance audio. By integrating optical music recognition and automatic music transcription modules, MuseAgent enables multi‑step reasoning and interaction over fine‑grained musical content.

The cleanest architecture‑pattern paper. M‑OMR + AMT + RAG + LLM = a system that answers "what key is this passage modulating to?" — which stock MLLMs fail at. 79.1% on MuseBench audio slice with GPT‑4.1 orchestrator. ICLR 2026 submission, no first‑party code yet.

Paper + OpenReview openreview.net/forum?id=kDqH5KmRWR
№ 05

VocalParse — Singing‑Voice Transcription with a Large Audio Language Model

Chen, Wang, Mu, Yang, Chng · 2026-05-06
We present VocalParse, a unified singing voice transcription (SVT) model built upon a Large Audio Language Model (LALM). Our novel contribution is to introduce an interleaved prompting formulation that jointly models lyrics, melody, and word‑note correspondence, yielding a generated sequence that directly maps to a structured musical score. Furthermore, we propose a Chain‑of‑Thought prompting strategy, which decodes lyrics first as a semantic scaffold.

Fine‑tunes Qwen3‑ASR‑1.7B to emit interleaved lyric + pitch + note + BPM tokens in one autoregressive pass. CoT: generate plain lyrics first, then the structured score — that's the robustness trick on OOD singing. SingCrawl pipeline for web‑scale data. SOTA on Opencpop/ACE‑KiSing/OpenSinger/PopCS. Code + weights on GitHub.

Code + weights + data github.com/pymaster17/VocalParse
№ 06

Text2Score — Sheet‑Music Generation with an LLM Planner

Bhandari et al. · v2 2026-07-29
We present Text2Score, a two‑stage framework comprising a planning stage and an execution stage for generating sheet music from natural language prompts. By deriving supervision signals directly from symbolic XML data, we propose an alternative to caption‑based training that bypasses noisy or scarce text‑music pairs. The LLM orchestrator emits a bar‑wise plan; a separate generator produces ABC notation conditioned on it.

Inverse‑AMT: text → score. Two‑stage: LLM plans musical structure bar‑by‑bar (key, time sig, instruments, harmony), smaller generator executes the notes. Beats pure‑LLM agent + 3 end‑to‑end baselines on expert eval. Full code + dataset + prompts open.

Code + dataset + prompts github.com/keshavbhandari/text2score
№ 07

Libretto — Symbolic Music for LLM Agents

Yichen Xu · UC Berkeley · 2026-06-21
We introduce Libretto, an agent‑facing framework for symbolic music generation and revision. Libretto uses an LLM‑native grammar with explicit onset slots, voices, and bar‑level organization, then evaluates each piece in a corpus‑calibrated statistical space over rhythm, harmony, melody, texture, form, and variation.

Output‑side peer to MuseAgent‑1. LLM‑native symbolic‑music grammar + corpus‑calibrated "music cloud" so the agent can retrieve, diagnose, and self‑revise generated MIDI. The companion that makes the AMT agent's edits auditable on musical‑structure axes. Paper only — no code yet.

Paper only No code link in abstract
№ 08

HeartMuLa v3 — Open‑Source Music Foundation Model Family

Yang, Xie, Yin, Wang, Yi, et al. · Zhejiang U. + ByteDance · v3 2026-07-07 v3 this cycle
We present a family of open‑source Music Foundation Models: (1) HeartCLAP, an audio‑text alignment model; (2) HeartTranscriptor, a robust lyric recognition model; (3) HeartCodec, a low‑frame‑rate (12.5 Hz) yet high‑fidelity music codec tokenizer; (4) HeartMuLa, an LLM‑based song generation model with 7B parameters.

Chinese‑academic Suno reproduction. HeartTranscriptor is the AMT‑adjacent piece — dedicated lyrics recognition as the vocal‑side counterpart to a Melodia/MT3‑style note transcriber. 12.5 Hz HeartCodec is the right frame‑rate regime for long‑form symbolic reasoning. v3 tightened the report. Direct intellectual predecessor to Qwen‑Music.

Open (gated weights) First-author GitHub: Dongchao Yang
№ 09 · Community benchmark

Advancing Multi‑Instrument Music Transcription — 2025 AMT Challenge Results

Chaturvedi et al. · 2026-03-29
This paper presents the results of the 2025 Automatic Music Transcription (AMT) Challenge, an online competition to benchmark progress in multi‑instrument transcription. Eight teams submitted valid solutions; two outperformed the baseline MT3 model. Top systems (MIROS F1 0.60, YourMT3‑YPTF‑MoE‑M 0.59) both use YourMT3‑style encoder–decoder Transformers with mixture‑of‑experts.

The community benchmark. Top systems are YourMT3‑style encoder‑decoder MoE transformers — no LLMs in the winner's circle. Pure token‑prediction still wins on a fixed eval; LALM orchestration wins on flexibility + OOD. Expect v2 to use MulTTiPop.

Challenge artifacts Per-team code varies; appendix of paper
№ 10 · Empirical justification

Evaluating Multimodal LLMs on Core Music Perception Tasks

Carone, Roman, Ripollés · NYU · NeurIPS 2025 WS NeurIPS 2025
Multimodal LLMs claim "musical understanding" via evaluations that conflate listening with score reading. We benchmark Gemini 2.5 Pro, Gemini 2.5 Flash, and Qwen2.5‑Omni across Syncopation Scoring, Transposition Detection, and Chord Quality Identification. Results reveal a clear perceptual gap: models perform near ceiling on MIDI but show accuracy drops on audio.

The cleanest empirical argument for why the AMT‑front‑end architecture (MuseAgent, VocalParse) exists at all. Most quotable line in the field right now: "current systems reason well over symbols (MIDI) but do not yet 'listen' reliably from audio." Every paper in this report except MuScriptor is downstream of this finding.
